# Acadium intern projects: DFW wedding launch

Two internship roles for the Dallas–Fort Worth launch of Something Clicks: **Market Development**, and **Marketing & Community**. Each has a 12-week plan with weekly assignments, deliverables and goals, plus the rules both interns follow when they talk to vendors or post about partners.

Assumptions to adjust when posting on Acadium: about 10 hours a week, 12 weeks, remote, with a 30-minute weekly check-in with Matt. Match the hours, length and any stipend to the terms in the Acadium posting.

---

## What the interns are launching

Something Clicks shows wedding businesses which local businesses fit theirs, and why. Owners claim their business for free, see their matches, request introductions (contact details are shared only when both sides accept) and set up referrals, shared offers or promotions.

In DFW today:

- About 5,200 wedding-specific businesses are mapped (venues, planners, photographers, florists, caterers and more), plus broad categories such as salons and bakeries that still need review.
- None of them are members until their owners claim and join. The interns' job is to change that, honestly.

**Launch goals (12 weeks).** These are planning targets to revise after week 4, not promises:

| Measure (from the admin) | Week 4 | Week 8 | Week 12 |
|---|---|---|---|
| DFW businesses claimed | 25 | 60 | 100 |
| Network members (joined) | 10 | 30 | 50 |
| Introductions accepted | 3 | 12 | 25 |
| Partnerships live | 1 | 5 | 10 |
| Referral partners (venues, planners) signed up | 3 | 8 | 12 |

Matt shares these numbers each Monday. Interns don't need admin access.

## Rules for both roles (read before week 1)

These rules exist for legal reasons and for trust. Breaking one is the only thing that ends an internship early. Not legal advice: **[Legal review]** before the posting goes live.

### 1. Never overstate what Something Clicks is

- A listed business is "not on Something Clicks yet". Never call it a member, a partner, or interested.
- Never say anyone is "waiting to connect" or "asked about you" unless they actually did.
- Use only live numbers from the site or Matt's Monday report. Round down ("240,000+").
- Price wording, word for word and nothing else: "Free to claim your business and see your matches."
- No invented testimonials, results, or "as seen in".

### 2. FTC disclosure: endorsements and partner promotions

The FTC's Endorsement Guides (16 CFR Part 255, revised 2023) require a clear disclosure whenever there is a **material connection** between the person promoting something and the business behind it. A material connection is anything a reader wouldn't expect that could affect how much weight they give the message.

**When interns post:**

- Anything an intern posts or says about Something Clicks, on any account including their personal ones, needs a disclosure, for example "I'm an intern at Something Clicks."
- An internship is a material connection, unpaid or not.

**When vendors promote each other through Something Clicks:**

- Cross-promotion between partner businesses is a material connection: referral swaps, shared offers, discounts, free services in return.
- When Business A shares Business B's offer, A must say that B is a partner.

**What a good disclosure looks like:**

- **Clear words:** "Our partner Bluebonnet Florals is offering…", "Partner offer", or "We have a referral partnership with…". Not "#sp", "#collab", or "thanks @…".
- **Hard to miss and next to the claim:** in the caption's first line or on the image. Not after "…more", not only in a bio or profile, not hidden in a block of hashtags.
- **Said out loud in video and live streams,** and repeated in a long one, in addition to any text.
- **Platform tools help but aren't enough on their own.** Use Instagram's or TikTok's paid-partnership label *and* the words.
- **Every post.** Not once per account.
- **Ads:** a partner's ad that mentions the other business is still a partner promotion and needs the same disclosure.

The dashboard reminds members of this on every live partnership. The interns also build a one-page **Partner Disclosure Guide** for members (Marketing & Community, week 3), with examples in this style.

### 3. Reviews and testimonials

The FTC's Rule on Consumer Reviews and Testimonials (16 CFR Part 465, effective October 21, 2024) bans fake or misleading reviews.

- Interns never write reviews of Something Clicks or of any vendor, anywhere.
- Never offer anything (discounts, features, priority) in exchange for a review, and never ask only happy members to review.
- Quotes on the site or social only come from real members, in their own words, with written permission. Note how they're connected to us, for example if they're also a referral partner.

### 4. Email, calls and texts

- **No bulk or cold email from interns.**
  - Invitation emails go out only from the Something Clicks system, in batches Matt approves, once the postal address is set (CAN-SPAM).
  - Interns may reply 1:1 to people who contacted us, or email someone they met who asked for information.
  - Any outreach template is Matt's approved one. It names Something Clicks, and stops the moment someone asks.
- **No texting businesses** that haven't texted us first or agreed to texts (TCPA).
- **Phone calls** to listed business numbers are fine when made by hand, during business hours. Say who you are and why you're calling. If they say "don't call", note it and Matt adds them to the suppression list the same day.

### 5. Data

- Interns don't get imported business email addresses, exports, or admin access.
- Work in the shared tracker only, never personal spreadsheets.
- Someone asks to be removed? Send them to somethingclicks.com/remove-my-business, or tell Matt the same day.

## Role 1: Market Development intern

**Purpose:** get DFW wedding businesses to claim their profile and join, and recruit the connectors (venue coordinators, planners) who bring others with them.

**Skills they'll build:** B2B prospecting, consultative calls, CRM discipline, partnership development, local market research.

**Weekly rhythm:** about 2 hours of research, 5 hours of calls and visits, 2 hours of tracker and notes, and the 30-minute check-in.

| Week | Assignment | Deliverable |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Learn the product end to end: claim a test business, request an introduction, and set up a partnership, using test businesses Matt sets up. Read the rules above. | Rules signed off. A one-page "how it works in my words" for Matt to check for accuracy. |
| 2 | Map the DFW wedding market by area (Dallas, Fort Worth, Plano/Frisco/McKinney, Arlington, Denton, Grapevine/Southlake) and category. Find the 40 businesses most likely to be hubs (busy venues, established planners). | DFW market map plus a prioritized list of 40 hub businesses with the reason for each. |
| 3 | Build the call script and objection notes with Matt ("Is this free?", "Where did you get my info?", "Who else is on it?"). Run 10 practice calls. | An approved script; 10 calls logged with outcomes. |
| 4 | Hub outreach: call the 40 hubs and offer a 15-minute walkthrough of their real matches. | 40 attempts, at least 10 walkthroughs booked. Week-4 numbers review. |
| 5 | Run walkthroughs: help owners claim live on the call (they verify by their own work email). | Walkthrough notes; claims attributed in the tracker. |
| 6 | Referral partners: pitch venue coordinators and planners on the referral-partner program (no payment promises; see the Terms). | 5 referral-partner conversations; at least 3 interest forms submitted. |
| 7 | Category gaps: find categories with few claims (for example DJs, rentals) and do targeted calls in those. | A gap report and 30 calls. |
| 8 | First introductions: for members who joined but haven't requested an introduction, call to help them pick 3 matches. | Introductions requested; a short note on why members hesitate. |
| 9 | In-person: attend 1–2 local wedding networking events or bridal shows (Matt approves which, and covers costs). Bring the one-page flyer. | Event recap, contacts logged, follow-ups done within 48 hours. |
| 10 | Partnerships: help connected pairs set up their first referral swap or shared offer, using the disclosure guide. | 3 or more partnerships proposed. |
| 11 | Feedback: 8 structured interviews (members and businesses that declined). | Interview summary with the top 5 product requests and their evidence. |
| 12 | Handoff and next metro: what worked, what didn't, and a playbook another intern could follow in Houston or Atlanta. | DFW playbook (5–8 pages) plus a final numbers review. |

**Done well looks like:** clean tracker entries the same day, honest pitches, and owners who claim because they saw their real matches, not because of pressure.

## Role 2: Marketing & Community intern

**Purpose:** make Something Clicks known and trusted among DFW wedding pros, give members what they need to promote partnerships properly, and build a community people want to be part of.

**Skills they'll build:** content strategy, social media for B2B, compliance-aware marketing, community programming, measurement.

**Weekly rhythm:** about 5 hours creating content, 2 hours on community and member support, 2 hours on measurement and planning, and the 30-minute check-in.

| Week | Assignment | Deliverable |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Learn the product and the rules above. Audit how 20 DFW wedding vendors present partners today (disclosures done well and badly). | Rules signed off. Audit deck with 10 good and bad disclosure examples (screenshots, names removed). |
| 2 | Voice and visuals: short, plain, benefit-first ("Good businesses know other good businesses."). Build post templates in the brand colors. | Approved messaging sheet and 6 post templates. |
| 3 | **Partner Disclosure Guide** for members: when to disclose, exact phrases, platform how-tos (Instagram, TikTok, Facebook, email, website), video rules. | A one-page guide plus a version for the help page, reviewed by Matt and sent for **[legal review]**. |
| 4 | Launch content: 2 weeks of posts explaining how matching works, using real categories and live counts only. | 8 scheduled posts; a disclosure line on any intern-personal posts. Week-4 numbers review. |
| 5 | "Who fits whom" series: short posts on why planners and florists, or venues and caterers, fit (the reasons the site shows). | 6 posts, plus engagement notes. |
| 6 | Member spotlights: with written permission, feature real members in their own words. Disclose any connection. | 2 spotlights with permission forms on file. |
| 7 | Community: plan a free DFW meetup (venue or coffee shop) for members and prospects to meet their matches. | Event plan, budget request, and an RSVP page draft. |
| 8 | Partnership kit: shareable assets members can use for a live partnership, with the disclosure built in ("Our partner … is offering …"). | A Canva kit with 5 templates, each with a disclosure line. |
| 9 | Run the meetup with Matt. Capture photos only with consent. | Event recap: attendance, introductions made on the day, follow-ups. |
| 10 | Opt-in newsletter: monthly, only to people who opted in, with an unsubscribe in every issue. | Newsletter template and first issue draft for Matt's approval. |
| 11 | Measurement: which posts and events led to claims (UTM links on everything). | Channel report: claims by source, cost, what to keep and cut. |
| 12 | Handoff: content calendar for the next quarter and a community playbook for the next metro. | Playbook plus 4 weeks of ready-to-schedule content. |

**Done well looks like:** every post disclosed correctly, no invented numbers, members using the kit and the guide, and a meetup people ask to repeat.

## Shared tools and check-ins

- **Tracker:** a shared sheet with columns for business, category, area, date, channel, outcome, next step, and do-not-contact. No email addresses from imported data.
- **Links:** every link an intern shares uses UTM tags (`utm_source=intern_md` or `intern_mc`, `utm_campaign=dfw_launch`) so claims can be attributed.
- **Check-in agenda (30 min):** numbers versus targets, blockers, one thing learned, the plan for next week.
- **Week 4 and week 8:** goals reviewed and reset with Matt based on real results.
